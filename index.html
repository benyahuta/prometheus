Chapter 1 · Tokenization

Text becomes tokens

A language model never sees letters. Input text is first broken into tokens — whole common words, or fragments of rarer ones — and each token is replaced by an integer ID. From here on, the model works only with those numbers.

Chapter 2 · Embeddings

Tokens become vectors

Each token ID indexes into a learned table and comes back as a long column of numbers — a vector in a high-dimensional space. GPT-3's vectors have 12,288 coordinates. Crucially, the geometry is meaningful: tokens with similar meanings point in similar directions.

One token, as a vector

Select a word. Each cell is one coordinate — blue negative, red positive. Words from the same family (cat, kitten) get visibly similar patterns.

24 of 12,288

A two-dimensional slice of embedding space

A projection of the space onto two of its directions. Every word is a vector from the origin. Drag any point — the readout shows its coordinates and its nearest neighbors by cosine similarity. Meaning clusters emerge on their own.

Select or drag a word to inspect its vector.

Directions in this space carry meaning. The classic example: king − man + woman ≈ queen.

Chapter 3 · Attention

Vectors talk to each other

So far every word is processed in isolation. Attention is the mechanism that lets each position look back at the others and pull in context — it's how an ambiguous word like it resolves what it refers to. Click any word below to see where its attention flows.

ending
attention head

Click it, then flip the ending between hungry and delicious. The attention jumps from robot to apple — context resolving reference. A real model runs dozens of heads like these in parallel, at every layer.

Chapter 5 · The output

A bet on the next token

All of that machinery exists to produce one thing: a probability for every token in the vocabulary. The temperature parameter reshapes that distribution before a token is drawn — low is conservative, high is adventurous.

“The cat sat on the ___”
1.00 balanced

Drag T low and sample repeatedly — it's mat almost every time. Drag it high and the tail of the distribution comes alive. This one dial is the difference between a careful assistant and a creative one.

That's the whole idea

Tokenize → embed → attend → stack → predict → sample → repeat. Scale it to billions of parameters and most of the internet, and this loop writes essays, code, and conversation. For the full mathematical story, watch 3Blue1Brown's transformer series, which inspired this page.
