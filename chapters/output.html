Softmax & sampling

The overview showed probability bars appearing out of nowhere. Here's the actual pipeline: raw scores called logits, the softmax function that turns them into probabilities, and the sampling strategies that pick the winner.

From vector to logits

After the last block, the final position's vector h is multiplied by one last matrix — the unembedding WU — producing one raw score per token in the vocabulary:

z = h · WU   (one number for each of ~50,000 tokens)

These logits can be any real number — negative, huge, whatever. They aren't probabilities yet. That's softmax's job.

The softmax function, live

pi = ezi / TΣj ezj / T

Drag the logits. Middle column: each score exponentiated. Right column: each share of the total — the probabilities. Notice softmax's character: it keeps order but exaggerates gaps, and no logit ever gets exactly zero.

1.00

Temperature divides every logit before the exponent. Small T stretches the gaps apart — the winner takes everything. Large T squashes the logits together — the distribution flattens toward uniform. Same scores, very different personalities.

Why exponentiate?

Three reasons. Positivity: ez is always positive, so every token keeps a nonzero chance. Order: it's monotonic, so a bigger logit always means a bigger probability. Sharpness: it's exponential, so a modest lead in logits becomes a decisive lead in probability — the model can express confidence. (And during training, its gradients are beautifully simple — no coincidence.)

How this connects to training

Training pushes these probabilities toward the truth. The loss for one position is cross-entropy: −log p(correct token). Guess the actual next token at 100%? Loss 0. Give it 1%? Loss is large. Every parameter you've met — embeddings, WQ, WK, WV, the MLPs, WU — is nudged, trillions of times, to make that one number smaller.
