Queries, Keys & Values

The overview showed attention as arcs between words. Here's what's actually computed. Every number on this page is a real matrix product — small enough to check by hand, arranged exactly like the real thing.

Three roles for every word

Each token's vector is projected three different ways, by three learned weight matrices:

Query Q — "what am I looking for?" The word ate might ask: "is there a noun around that could be doing the eating?"
Key K — "what do I contain?" The word robot advertises: "I'm an animate noun."
Value V — "what will I hand over if you attend to me?" The information that actually flows once a match is made.

A query matching a key is a dot product — large when the two vectors point the same way. The whole attention pattern falls out of one formula:

Attention(Q, K, V) = softmax QKT√dk V

Let's compute it, step by step, for the sentence "the robot ate apples".

Step 1 — the input X

Four tokens, each a 4-dimensional embedding (tiny on purpose; GPT-3 uses 12,288). One row per token:

X  (4 tokens × 4 dims)

Step 2 — project into Q, K, V

Multiply X by each of the three learned matrices. These weights are what training actually learns — everything downstream is just arithmetic:

Q = X·WQ
what each word seeks
K = X·WK
what each word offers
V = X·WV
what each word hands over

Step 3 — scores: every query meets every key

S = QKT/√dk. Row i, column j answers: how well does word i's question match word j's advertisement? The √dk keeps the numbers from blowing up as vectors get longer. Watch row ate — its query lines up with robot's key.

a word may not look at words that come after it — that would be cheating during training
S = QKT/√dk
rows: query word · columns: key word

Step 4 — softmax turns scores into weights

Each row is pushed through a softmax, so it becomes a set of positive weights summing to 100%. Masked positions (−∞) get exactly 0. This is the attention pattern — the numbers behind the arcs on the main page.

A = softmax(S)
each row sums to 100%

Step 5 — collect the values

Finally each word's output is a weighted average of everyone's value vectors: O = A·V. Because ate put most of its weight on robot, its output row is mostly robot's value — the verb now "knows" who is doing the eating.

O = A·V
the updated token vectors

And then: many heads at once

That was one head. A real layer runs many in parallel — GPT-3 runs 96 — each with its own WQ, WK, WV, so each can hunt for something different: one head tracks who-did-what, another tracks adjectives, another just watches the previous word. The heads' outputs are concatenated and mixed by one more learned matrix:

MultiHead(X) = concat(head1, …, headh) · WO

Everything above happens at every layer, for every token, in parallel — one big batched matrix multiplication. That's why GPUs, which are matrix-multiplying machines, are the hardware of choice.
